## Headline consensus (all ten reports)

Every report that touched architecture reached the same core conclusion
independently:

> **Make a coarse, canonical history authoritative, and make every finer level
> a pure, seeded refinement *conditioned on* it that may add detail but never
> contradict or write back.**

They also agreed on four supporting points:

- Derive every random draw from a **structured key** such as
  `(seed, entity, level, bucket, purpose, ordinal)`. Never use a shared or
  chained stream.
- Keep **digested state in integers or fixed-point**. Transcendental `Math.*`
  functions are not bit-exact across JS engines.
- **Sort explicitly.** Never let `Map`, `Set` or object key order, or
  discovery order, reach state.
- Keep a **causal layer** (numbers that drive consequences) separate from a
  **narrative layer** (chronicle, myth, propaganda).

No source shows a game or model that already guarantees "arbitrary query order
and optional fine levels never change coarse history". Astra and Sonnet both
say this guarantee will probably be novel to the Lab, and that it must be
enforced by tests rather than assumed.

## Topic 1: History generators

### Merged findings

**Dwarf Fortress (DF)**

1. **DF world generation is a sequential "zero-player strategy game".** It runs
   over a user-set history length and records typed events (wars, site
   foundings, deaths). The seed is logged so the world can be reproduced.
   Sources: https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/World_generation,
   https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Advanced_world_generation.
   Confidence: medium (wiki via WebFetch summary). [S]
   - The specific numbers are **unconfirmed**: a maximum of 2000 years, an
     `END_YEAR` of up to 10000, 3 to 160 civilisations, an error after 86
     rejections, and the 34% / 67% megabeast checks for Age of Legends and Age
     of Heroes. Astra fetched the advanced-worldgen page but found no primary
     confirmation of modern caps, the history timestep or the rejection
     thresholds.
2. **DF rejection can loop forever.** The July 2008 dev log describes infinite
   rejection loops, controls for minimum-value rejection, and a "bypass
   rejects" option. Tarn Adams warns that the bypass option breaks
   reproducibility. Source: https://www.bay12games.com/dwarves/dev_2008.html
   (July 18-19). Confidence: high for the problem. [S+A]
   - Proposed remedy (Astra, medium): draw a bounded list of candidates as
     `candidate_i = G(H(seed,'candidate',i))`, record why each one is
     rejected, and return a deterministic failure or a versioned fallback.
     Never retry until a wall-clock deadline.
3. **DF keeps the simulated population separate from recorded history.**
   - Culling dead historical figures and their events cut peak memory from
     about 700 MB to about 200 MB (dev log, 10 June 2008).
   - In 2015 routine annual festivals were left out of tracked history, while
     important festivals were still recorded (dev log, 2 April 2015).
   - Sources: https://www.bay12games.com/dwarves/dev_2008.html,
     https://www.bay12games.com/dwarves/dev_2015.html. Confidence: high (a
     historical developer report, not a benchmark). [A]
4. **Long DF histories cost hours, and civilisations can die out before play
   starts.** Sources:
   https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/World_generation (medium) and
   https://www.bay12games.com/media/df_talk_combined_transcript.html (Tarn
   reports thousand-year generations taking an hour or more; high for the
   report). [S+A]

**Caves of Qud**

5. **Caves of Qud generates events first and rationalises their causes
   afterwards** (Grinblat and Bucklew, FDG 2017).
   - There are five sultan periods. Each biography starts from an initialised
     entity, runs one event, then about twelve more, and adds a death event if
     needed.
   - Causes are rationalised from the actor's current state. If no cause
     fits, the event invents one by editing that state.
   - The authors explicitly do not simulate historical logic.
   - Source: https://pcgworkshop.com/archive/grinblat2017subverting.pdf
     (sections 4.1-4.6). Confidence: high (read in full by both). [S+A]
6. **Narrative glue in Qud** comes from two devices.
   - A small **shared state vector** on each entity (factions, profession,
     domains, regions, named items) that every event reads and writes.
   - Seeded thematic **domains** (for example ice, scholarship, chance) that
     thread a theme through a whole life.
   - History is stored as discrete events in a Tracery-like grammar, and named
     places and items become world content later.
   - Sources: the same paper (sections 4.2, 4.4, 4.6) and
     https://github.com/galaxykate/tracery. Confidence: high. [S]

**Ultima Ratio Regum**

7. **Ultima Ratio Regum** has religions that span one or several nations.
   Cultural generation reaches from national ideology down to artifacts. In
   2016 Johnson said its history generation "needed reworking". Source:
   https://heiup.uni-heidelberg.de/journals/religions/article/download/23550/17274/0
   (pp. 97-98). Confidence: high for the interview, medium for the inference.
   No URR history timestep or population cap was verified. [A] (Sonnet's URR
   fetch returned 404.)

**Talk of the Town**

8. **Talk of the Town** spends detailed social simulation only on selected
   timesteps.
   - It runs from 19 Aug 1839 to 19 Aug 1979, with two potential timesteps per
     day.
   - Each potential timestep becomes detailed with probability
     `p = 10/(365*2)`, about ten per year on average. A code comment gives 3.6
     as the Bad News setting.
   - Births and business processing run on non-detailed steps too.
   - Sources:
     https://raw.githubusercontent.com/james-owen-ryan/talktown/master/config/basic_config.py,
     https://raw.githubusercontent.com/james-owen-ryan/talktown/master/simulation.py.
     Confidence: high. [A]
   - Sonnet noted Ryan's finding that emergent knowledge simulation produced
     trivia (characters talking about hair and eye colour), and that his later
     work curated the output instead. Source:
     https://emshort.blog/2019/05/28/curating-simulated-storyworlds-james-ryan-ch-6f/.
     Confidence: medium. [S]

**Paradox games (CK2, CK3, Stellaris, Victoria 3)**

9. **Paradox event scheduling is pulse- and trigger-driven.**
   - CK3 uses on_actions plus yearly, quarterly and five-year pulses. There is
     no monthly pulse, "for performance reasons". Selection modes are
     all-fire, weighted and first-valid. The CK3 docs warn that nested
     `every_living_character` loops cause about 200,000 operations. Source:
     https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Event_modding (medium). [S]
   - Stellaris uses `is_triggered_only` and gatekeeper events, cheap early
     triggers, and explicit delayed chains. The wiki says vanilla is removing
     MTTH (mean time to happen) for performance. Source:
     https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Event_modding (medium). [S]
   - The CK2 schema keeps trigger, `is_triggered_only`,
     `mean_time_to_happen`, `weight_multiplier`, `immediate`, options and AI
     modifiers as separate fields. Source:
     https://github.com/cwtools/cwtools-ck2-config/blob/master/event/events.cwt
     (high for the schema, medium for engine behaviour). [A]
   - Typed event context comes from two places. A Stellaris on_actions
     template gives each hook its own scopes. Victoria 3 journal entries have
     pulses, saved scopes, completion, failure and invalidation handlers, and
     an example 720-day timeout. Sources:
     https://gist.github.com/ttowncompiled/7faa311431acbbb6841700eab850a4c8,
     https://github.com/Victoria-3-Modding-Co-op/Modding-Digests/blob/main/1.12.0/types/journal_entries.md.
     Confidence: medium. [A]
10. **The MTTH maths must be written out explicitly** (Astra's derivation, not
    an engine formula). If M is a median waiting time, use
    `p(dt) = 1 - 2^(-dt/M)`. If mu is a mean waiting time, use
    `p(dt) = 1 - e^(-dt/mu)`.
    - A constant hazard composes across intervals, so the distribution is the
      same at every resolution. The seeded outcomes are not the same, though:
      that needs canonical event times or conditioned refinement.
    - Confidence: high for the derivation, unverified for any engine's MTTH
      semantics. [A]

**Dominions 6**

11. **Dominions 6** chains events with numeric codes attached to provinces.
    - The manual warns that code collisions scramble chains. It reserves -300
      to -5000 for modders, and 0 means reset.
    - Guards include uniqueness counts, earliest and latest turn, and
      `#req_rare`. `#req_rare` is a validity probability checked when the
      event is rolled, not a per-turn rate.
    - Source: https://illwinter.com/dom6/dom6eventman.pdf (v6.29).
      Confidence: high. [A]

**Story sifting (Felt and Winnow)**

12. **Felt and Winnow** are story-sifting tools by Max Kreminski.
    - Felt keeps entity-attribute-value facts in DataScript and matches them
      with Datalog patterns that support joins, negation and recursion. It
      separates reflection actions (which produce intent tokens) from external
      actions (which consume them).
    - Winnow does incremental matching with negative constraints. Its
      `tryAdvance` forks partial matches and keeps the original.
    - Winnow's published benchmark: 912 ms per event with 1,000 partial
      matches, against 13 ms with ten (2019 laptop and browser).
    - Sources:
      https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Felt_SimpleStorySifter.pdf,
      https://github.com/mkremins/felt,
      https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mkremins/winnow/master/runner.js,
      https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Winnow_AIIDE2021.pdf.
      Confidence: high for the mechanisms, medium for the adaptation. [A]

**Political geography and tech trees**

13. **Turchin et al.** model political geography on 100 x 100 km cells with
    binary cultural and military traits, conquest, diffusion and dissolution.
    Every border cell gets one attack opportunity per year, processed in random
    order. The empirical comparison samples every 100 years, which is not the
    simulation step. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3799307/.
    Confidence: high for the mechanics. [A] (See Topic 2 for the equations.)
14. **Tech prerequisite structure.**
    - Freeciv separates research prerequisites (`req1`, `req2`) from
      acquisition restrictions (`root_req`), and its tree is hand-authored.
      Source:
      https://raw.githubusercontent.com/freeciv/freeciv/main/data/classic/techs.ruleset.
    - Heinimaki's thesis supports alternative prerequisite sets and gives cost
      estimation without enumerating every research order (Algorithms 1-2,
      around PDF p.169). Source:
      https://trepo.tuni.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/212812/heinimaki_1349.pdf?sequence=-1.
    - Confidence: high for the structures. [A]
15. **Worlds (Zematus)** keeps dated events in a tree and rejects stale or
    invalid entries when they come up for execution. Source:
    https://github.com/Zematus/Worlds/blob/main/Assets/Scripts/WorldEngine/World.cs.
    Confidence: high for the inspected methods. Astra did not establish that
    the whole engine is query-order independent. [A]
16. **Seshat has a single dominant complexity component.**
    - 414 societies, 51 variables, nine characteristics. PC1 explains about
      three quarters of the variance (about 77% in a later study).
    - Sonnet had this from a search snippet only (low). Astra confirmed it from
      https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29269395/ and
      https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-16035-9 (high).
    - Sonnet's snippet also gave the span "over 10,000 years"; that was not
      confirmed. [S+A]

### Where Sonnet and Astra agree

- Use a causal or canonical layer plus a separate, rationalised narrative
  layer. Qud is the model for the narrative layer and must not become the
  causal one.
- DF-style full sequential simulation is costly and is **not** query-order
  independent.
- Rejection sampling must be bounded and keyed only by the seed.
- Schedule events through pulses and triggers, and never poll every entity on
  every fine tick.
- Curate or threshold what enters the chronicle. Emergent detail is mostly
  mundane.

### Where they conflict or differ

- **Talk of the Town code.** Sonnet said "code repositories not located".
  Astra found and read `james-owen-ryan/talktown` (MIT). *Resolved in Astra's
  favour.*
- **DF numbers.** Sonnet reports specific caps from wiki summaries (medium).
  Astra explicitly declined to promote them to facts. *Treat them as
  unverified until someone reads the wiki directly.*
- **MTTH.** Sonnet reports Stellaris removing MTTH for performance (wiki
  summary). Astra warns that MTTH semantics and polling intervals are
  version-specific and unverified. These do not contradict each other. *Do
  not copy the MTTH label; write the waiting-time law explicitly.*

  summaries only).
- Stellaris late-game weakness (search snippets of reviews and forum posts;

- Seshat's licence (Sonnet "believed CC", from memory). See the conflict in

- No source covered Songs of Syx scheduling, Dominions history depth, or Civ

## Topic 2: Quantitative history

### Merged findings

**Population and carrying capacity**

1. **Logistic population with carrying capacity K.**
   - Model: `dN/dt = rN(1 - N/K)`. For constant r and K, the exact update is
     `N(t+h) = K / (1 + (K/N(t) - 1) e^{-rh})`.
   - Agrarian r is about 1 to 2% per year in Turchin's cycle examples.
   - Sources:
     https://www.imsc.res.in/~sitabhra/meetings/sgn24/assets/presentations/Turchin_2009_AnnNYAS_Long_term_population_cycles_in_human_societies.pdf
     [A], https://github.com/jbennettgit/NADSM_PLOSONE (`Population.py`) [S].
     Confidence: high.
   - NADSM adds a **K-crash** rule: if P > K, the population drops at once to
     delta times K. Its net birth rate is 1.75% per year under Campbell. [S]
2. **Brander-Taylor two-stock renewable economy.**
   - Model: `dS/dt = rS(1 - S/K) - αβSL` and `dL/dt = L[(B-D) + φαβS]`.
   - Decade-unit baseline: K = 12000, r = 0.04, B-D = -0.1, α = 1e-5,
     β = 0.4, φ = 4, S0 = 12000, L0 = 40. These come from a reproduction; the
     original 1998 text was not verified.
   - Source:
     https://www.researchgate.net/publication/24057902_Could_institutional_reform_have_saved_Easter_Island.
     Confidence: high. [A]
3. **HANDY: commoners, elites, nature and wealth.** HANDY has four stocks
   (commoners, elites, nature and wealth) and full equations.
   - Baseline: α_m = .01, α_M = .07, β = .03 per year, s = .0005, ρ = .005,
     γ = .01, λ = 100, κ = 1, 10 or 100.
   - Because γλ = 1 per year, resources recover far faster than people
     reproduce, so decade-length Euler steps are unsafe.
   - Source:
     https://www.researchgate.net/publication/261291435_Human_and_nature_dynamics_HANDY_Modeling_inequality_and_use_of_resources_in_the_collapse_or_sustainability_of_societies.
     Confidence: high. [A]

**Political instability and elites**

4. **The demographic-fiscal SDT model** (structural-demographic theory: an
   instability index built from mass hardship, elite overproduction and fiscal
   distress). Its stocks are N, S and W, and K(W) = k_max - cW. Figure 7
   values: r0 = .015 and others. The reproduction has notation gaps (c and d
   versus δ), and there is a singularity at K(W) → 0. Source: the Turchin 2009
   PDF above. Confidence: medium. [A] Sonnet reached the same idea through the
   Qing analysis (https://github.com/seshatdb/QingCollapse, CC0), but did not
   read the formulas (medium). [S+A]
5. **Political Stress Indicator** `Psi = MMP × EMP × SFD`.
   - MMP (mass mobilisation potential) = `u·A_20-29 / w`.
   - EMP (elite mobilisation potential) = `E / (ε·s·N)`.
   - SFD (state fiscal distress) = `(Y/G)·D`.
   - Elite share follows `de/dt = μ0(1 - w/w0)`, with μ0 = .002 per year.
   - **Psi is not a collapse probability.**
   - Sources: https://escholarship.org/content/qt6qp8x28p/qt6qp8x28p.pdf,
     https://escholarship.org/content/qt1gs8r11g/qt1gs8r11g.pdf.
     Confidence: high. [A]
6. **NADSM elite misery triggers collapse into factions.**
   - Factions get boosted asabiya (group cohesion) for about one generation,
     then re-collapse with odds 0.25.
   - Territories are sorted by `t_index` into a canonical order when factions
     are created. That is the right pattern for removing iteration-order
     dependence.
   - Source: https://github.com/jbennettgit/NADSM_PLOSONE (`NADSM.py`).
     Confidence: high. [S]

**Conquest, power and hierarchy**

7. **Power projection over distance.** Two forms of the same idea, from two
   sources:
   - **NADSM [S]:** Boulding `1/(1+1/s)^d`, or Turchin's approximation
     `exp(-d/s)`, with s about 1 to 3. Source: NADSM (high).
   - **Turchin 2013 empire ABM [A]:** `P = 1 + β·Ū·S (+ γ·E_d)`,
     `p_win = max(0, (P_a - P_d)/(P_a + P_d))`, plus split and replace
     probabilities. Military traits diffuse and are never lost. Source:
     https://www.kokkonuts.org/wp-content/uploads/PNAS-2013-Turchin-16384-9.pdf.
     Confidence: high for the equations; the **coefficient table was not
     verified**.
   - A third related form: Griffin-Stanish use `F(D) = F(0)/(1+f)^D` with
     f = .10 per km and a defence multiplier of 2.1 (see item 9).
8. **Gavrilets-Anderson-Turchin chiefdom cycling.**
   - Conflict: `p = F_i^α / (F_i^α + F_j^α)`.
   - Tribute: a fraction θ of resources goes up the hierarchy. Each superior
     has at most L direct subordinates. Chief tenure τ is 5 to 20 years.
   - Peaceful recovery is exact: `f(t+h) = f0 - [f0 - f(t)]·2^{-h/r}`.
   - Source:
     https://escholarship.org/content/qt5536t55r/qt5536t55r.pdf?t=pii20r.
     Confidence: high. [A]
9. **Griffin-Stanish settlement ABM.** Force decay (see item 7), a
   labour-limited farmed area, producer fractions of .70 and .25, growth of
   0.18% per year, and a five-year food memory. Source:
   https://www.researchgate.net/publication/26492419_An_Agent-Based_Model_of_Prehistoric_Settlement_Patterns_and_Political_Consolidation_in_the_Lake_Titicaca_Basin_of_Peru_and_Bolivia.
   Confidence: high. These coefficients are on a km scale. [A]

**Complexity, innovation and energy**

10. **Tainter's diminishing returns, formalised.**
    - Model: `L = (N - A)(1 - ρ)^A`, administrative cost `e = L^a + c·C^b`,
      and growth of the administrative workforce A as in the source.
    - Defaults: N = 400, ρ = .02, a = b = .75, c = 1.05, β = 15.
    - Steps are abstract; there is no calibration in years.
    - Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2102.06698. Confidence: high. [A]
    - Sonnet had Tainter only from memory (unverified).
11. **Kremer 1993: population-driven innovation.**
    - Technology grows as `dA/dt = g·N·A`. Under Malthusian adjustment,
      `dN/dt = (g/(1-α))N²`.
    - The exact reduction blows up in finite time, which means the regime has
      to hand off to something else; it is not a numerical bug.
    - Kremer tested the model on isolated land masses.
    - Sources: https://www.steveambler.uqam.ca/511/readings/kremer.1993.pdf
      [A], https://stafforini.com/works/kremer-1993-population-growth-technological/,
      https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/qjecon/v108y1993i3p681-716..html [S].
      Confidence: high. [S+A]
12. **World3 capital and delay modules.**
    - Capital follows `dI/dt = J - I/τ_I`, with τ_I = 14 years. Its exact
      frozen-J update is `I(t+h) = I e^{-h/τ} + Jτ(1 - e^{-h/τ})`.
    - A first-order delay is stable under Euler for h < 2τ.
    - Sources: https://github.com/cvanwynsberghe/pyworld3 and its
      `capital.py`, `world3.py` and `specials.py`. Confidence: high. [S+A]
13. **Morris's energy index.** `S_E = 250·e/230000`, with e in kcal per person
    per day. Simple hunter-gatherers are at about 4000 to 5000; the US in 2000
    was at 230000. Source: https://pzacad.pitzer.edu/~lyamane/ianmorris.pdf.
    Confidence: high. [A]
    - Smil gives historical argument rather than an equation. Source:
      https://vaclavsmil.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/WEF_EN_IndustryVision-12.pdf.
      [A]
    - **[Opus note]** 4000 kcal/day is about 6.1 GJ per person per year, and
      230000 kcal/day is about 351 GJ. That is consistent with Sonnet's
      from-memory "forager 5-10 GJ" and higher than its vague "~100+
      industrial".
14. **Kardashev-Sagan interpolation.** `K = (log10(P/W) - 6)/10`. Use it as a
    display metric only: aggregate power first, and never average K values.
    Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.5455. Confidence: high. [A] (Sonnet:
    from memory.)

**Diamond, and calibration data**

15. **Diamond as connectivity, not destiny.** A quantitative test does **not**
    find privileged Eurasian east-west corridors. Source:
    https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/evolutionary-human-sciences/article/geography-is-not-destiny-a-quantitative-test-of-diamonds-axis-of-orientation-hypothesis/3196029E586CC58C6696D5AB9994ADF7.
    Confidence: high. [A]
    - Astra proposes a transfer hazard
      `λ_ij = λ0·exp[-a·d_ij - b·ΔClimate_ij - c·barrier_ij]`. This is its own
      equation, not Diamond's, and has no coefficients.
16. **Seshat as calibration data.** Equinox and other Seshat datasets serve as
    a dev-time check and an observation operator, never as a growth law.
    Sources: https://github.com/seshatdb/Equinox_Data,
    https://seshat-global-history-databank.readthedocs.io/en/latest/research_code.html
    (an index of ten papers with code). Confidence: high. [S+A]
17. **Neolithic boom and bust model** (Kondor et al. 2022). Spatial cells,
    fitted to radiocarbon data. Source:
    https://github.com/dkondor/neolithic_simulation (GPL-3.0). Confidence:
    medium. [S]

### Where they agree

- A small stock-and-flow core with closed-form updates (logistic, capital
  decay, delays) is usable at coarse steps.
- NADSM, Seshat and Turchin's models are prior art to mine for equations, not
  code to copy, because of their RNG, ordering and licences.
- Contested theories (SDT, secular cycles) should be **parameterised event

## Topic 3: Multi-scale determinism

### Merged findings

**The consistency law, and when a coarse model is exact**

1. **Consistency as an executable projection law: `A(R(H_c, k)) = H_c`.**
   - It applies to whole histories, including event identities and dates.
   - "Preserves the recorded constraints" is achievable by construction.
     "Samples the exact conditional distribution of the fine model" needs a
     compatible joint model.
   - Sources: https://www.anandnatrajan.com/papers/TOMACS97.pdf,
     https://www.anandnatrajan.com/papers/PADS97.pdf [A];
     https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.5108 (search snippet only, medium) [S].
     Confidence: high. [S+A]
   - Sonnet's form is "aggregation is easy, disaggregation is hard" (snippet
     from
     https://ifaamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2013/docs/p159.pdf),
     paired with a round-trip test on each level edge.
2. **Strong lumpability decides whether an aggregate can evolve on its own**
   (whether a coarse state variable is a true simplification of the fine
   chain, or only an approximation). Source:
   https://doi.org/10.1007/s12597-025-00969-z. Confidence: high. [A]

**Splitting totals, placing events and filling paths**

3. **Conserved splitting of integer totals.**
   - Use `Multinomial(N; p)`, or a binary tree of `N_L ~ Binomial(N, p_L)`
     with `N_R = N - N_L`, optionally with Dirichlet weights. Key each split on
     a frozen partition tree.
   - Source: https://web.stanford.edu/class/stats366/Mixt.html. Confidence:
     high. [A]
   - Sonnet had this from memory (low) and found it consistent with
     https://simulation.tudelft.nl/SEN9110/slides/12_MultiResolutionSimulation/SEN9110_12_MultiResolutionSimulation.pdf
     (not read in detail). [S+A]
4. **Place events conditionally once their count is fixed.** Given N events
   in an interval, the times are sorted uniforms, or a hierarchical binomial
   on a fixed time tree. Source:
   https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-262-discrete-stochastic-processes-spring-2011/ecdf61bc480eb9eebbc9024ea7d15091_ct0QGoi3n4Q.pdf.
   Confidence: high. [A]
5. **Bridge trees for continuous latent variables** (a Virtual Brownian Tree).
   - The midpoint given its endpoints is
     `N((X_a + X_b)/2, σ²(b - a)/4)`.
   - Unsuitable for population as it stands: clipping a Gaussian path changes
     the distribution.
   - Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.06464,
     https://raw.githubusercontent.com/patrick-kidger/diffrax/main/diffrax/_brownian/tree.py.
     Confidence: high. [A]
6. **Endpoint-conditioned Markov paths for regime and tech stages.** Use
   `P_aj(t)·P_jb(T-t) / P_ab(T)` or uniformization. Source:
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.1683. Confidence: high for the maths, medium for
   an efficient deterministic JS implementation. [A]
7. **Refine population flows jointly.** Use
   `P_{t+1} = P_t + B - D + I - E`. Migration is one shared transfer record.
   Source: PADS97. Confidence: medium (a design inference). [A]
8. **Tau-leaping.** Replace many exact events with Poisson(rate·τ), with a
   bounded leap and protection against negative counts. Keep rare
   catastrophic events exact. Source:
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tau-leaping. Confidence: medium. [S]

**Random number generation**

9. **Counter-based RNG addressed semantically.**
   - `key = derive(seed, modelVersion, entityId, level, bucket, purpose)`,
     and `bits = RNG(key, decisionOrdinal, rejectionAttempt)`.
   - Tuples must be encoded unambiguously, because concatenation can alias two
     different keys.
   - Sources:
     https://www.thesalmons.org/john/random123/papers/random123sc11.pdf [S],
     https://www.cs.tufts.edu/~nr/cs257/archive/john-salmon/parallel-random.pdf
     [A], https://docs.jax.dev/en/latest/jep/263-prng.html [A]. Confidence:
     high. [S+A]
10. **Chained SplitMix splits are order-dependent.** Derive streams by hashing
    the path, not by splitting on demand. Sources:
    https://gee.cs.oswego.edu/dl/papers/oopsla14.pdf [S+A],
    https://research.chalmers.se/publication/183348 (abstract only) [S].
    Confidence: medium (S) / high (A).
11. **RNG family options for JS.**
    - SplitMix with BigInt.
    - Philox, which needs the high 32-bit word of a wide multiply.
    - Threefry, which uses only add, rotate and XOR.
    - PCG32 for local streams.
    - Squirrel noise as a hash, but with no proof of statistical independence.
    - Validate whichever is chosen against reference vectors.
    - Sources:
      https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DEShawResearch/random123/main/include/Random123/philox.h,
      https://www.pcg-random.org/using-pcg-c-basic.html,
      https://gist.github.com/kevinmoran/0198d8e9de0da7057abe8b8b34d50f86.
      Confidence: high for the mechanics, medium for the JS preference. [A]

**Event ordering and time stepping**

12. **Order events by semantic identity.** The key is
    `(timeNs, causalMicrostep, phasePriority, ownerId, eventId)`.
    - Simultaneous actions read one pre-state, then resolve conflicts and
      commit together.
    - SimPy's `(time, increasing id)` ordering is deterministic only if the
      scheduling order is stable.
    - Source:
      https://simpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/topical_guides/time_and_scheduling.html.
      Confidence: high. [A]
13. **DEVS-style components with fixed multirate coupling.** Never change the
    authoritative step when the user zooms. Sources:
    https://web.ornl.gov/~nutarojj/adevs/docs/manual/node5.html,
    https://sundials.readthedocs.io/en/v7.7.0/arkode/Mathematics_link.html.
    Confidence: high. [A]
    - Sonnet's "hierarchical time stepping with a canonical event queue,
      where a finer level refines only timing and participants" is the same
      idea, from memory (low). [S+A]

**Replay, laziness and verification**

14. **Event sourcing with complete checkpoints.** Record resolved outcomes,
    not "roll again" instructions. A checkpoint does not make a cold query far
    into the future cheap. Source:
    https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html. Confidence: high.
    [S+A]
15. **Laziness is a property of the dependency graph.** Interactions that
    cross regions need one canonical owner. Beware *chain disaggregation*,
    where one detailed interaction forces ever larger regions into detail.
    Source: PADS97. Confidence: high. [A]
16. **Use lockstep debugging discipline.** Treat alternative query schedules
    (coarse-first versus fine-first, shuffled, warm versus evicted cache) as
    separate lockstep clients and compare their digests.
    - Sources:
      https://www.gamedevs.org/uploads/1500-archers-age-of-empires-network-programming.pdf
      [A], https://gafferongames.com/post/deterministic_lockstep/ [S].
      Confidence: high. [S+A]
17. **Canonical serialisation plus Merkle digests, with an honest scope.**
    Keep three things separate: the recipe commitment, the result digest, and
    the materialisation record. Sources:
    https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html,
    https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8785.html. Confidence: high. [A] (Sonnet:
    the same idea from memory.)

**JS arithmetic hazards** (Gaffer, plus the ECMAScript specification). Confidence:
high. [S+A]

- `Math.sin`, `exp` and `pow` are implementation-approximated.
- BigInt division truncates toward zero.
- Above 2^53 a Number is no longer an exact integer.
- Float reassociation changes sums.
- Sources: https://gafferongames.com/post/floating_point_determinism/,
  https://tc39.es/ecma262/multipage/numbers-and-dates.html,
  https://tc39.es/ecma262/multipage/ecmascript-data-types-and-values.html.

### Where they agree

- The coarse level is authoritative and fine levels are stateless and
  regenerable.
- Observation must never mutate state.
- Use path-keyed counter-based RNG.
- Use integer or fixed-point state and canonical ordering.
- Snapshots are caches that can be rebuilt from the seed.
- Test with a round-trip or projection check per level edge, plus digest
  equality across query orders and across which levels were realised.

### Where they conflict or differ

- **Philox in JS.** Sonnet suggests "implement Philox4x32 with `Math.imul`
  and 32-bit ops". Its own open question adds "via 16-bit halves". Astra
  warns that `Math.imul` returns **only the low 32 bits**, and Philox needs
  the high word. *Astra is right that `Math.imul` alone is insufficient. A
  multiply-high built from 16-bit halves or from BigInt is required. Validate
  against Random123 reference vectors.*
- **Exact versus distributional agreement.** Sonnet allows coarse and fine to
  match "exactly, or in distribution". Astra insists the projection law holds
  exactly on declared constraints, and that distributional agreement is a
  separate claim that needs lumpability or a joint model. *Not a
  contradiction, but the Lab must declare per quantity which guarantee it
  gives. Sonnet raised the same open question.*

## Topic 4: Space expansion and extinction

### Merged findings

**How expansion spreads**

1. **Finite-range settlement front (the Aurora Effect).**
   - Front speed is `v_f = d / (d/v_p + T_p)`.
   - Illustrative parameters: 0.01c, a 10 ly range, a 100-year launch period
     and 0.08 pc^-3. These give v_f ≈ 0.0091c, about 11 Myr across 100,000 ly
     (Astra's arithmetic).
   - With finite civilisation lifetimes the galaxy reaches a **steady state**
     with many civilisations and still-unsettled gaps.
   - Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04450 /
     https://arxiv.org/html/1902.04450v2. Confidence: high. [S+A]
2. **Settlement death and recolonisation.** The mean-field equilibrium is
   `X* = 1 - T_l/T_s`, or `(T_s - T_l)/(T_s + t_p)` with an in-transit stock.
   Use it as an aggregate check only, not as an occupancy oracle. Same source.
   Confidence: high. [A]
3. **Landis percolation.** Each colony is a coloniser with probability P,
   within a maximum range. This produces clusters and voids. Source:
   https://ntrs.nasa.gov/search.jsp?R=19940022867. Confidence: high for the
   mechanism, low for any universal threshold. [A]
4. **Fisher-KPP as a benchmark.** `v_min = 2√(rD)`. Sources:
   https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022519312004730,
   https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19790011801 (Newman-Sagan). **Astra's fetch
   of both failed; only search-visible content was used.** Confidence: medium.
   [A]
5. **Separate exploration from colonisation (Cotta and Morales).** Probes
   explore much faster than settlement spreads. There are about 10^2 to 10^3
   simultaneous exploring civilisations under a 50 Myr probe lifetime and
   1 Myr evidence persistence. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.0345
   (abstract; the PDF did not parse). Confidence: high for the abstract
   claims. [S]

**Civilisation lifetime and the Great Filter**

6. **Lifetime L dominates.** The number of coexisting civilisations is about
   birth rate times L. Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.04802. Confidence:
   medium. [S]
7. **Wide priors (Sandberg, Drexler and Ord).** Many seeds should be empty.
   One model uses a log-uniform communicating lifetime from 1e2 to 1e10 years.
   Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02404,
   https://arxiv.org/html/1806.02404v1 (Table 1). Confidence: high. [S+A]
8. **The Great Filter as ordered transitions.** Waiting time per stage is
   `W_j = -ln(U_j)/k_j`, and histories that overrun the habitability window are
   rejected. Hanson distinguishes one-time opportunities from repeatable
   steps. Sources: https://mason.gmu.edu/~rhanson/greatfilter.html,
   https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.2222 (Forgan's testbed). Confidence: high for
   the framing, low for the numerical priors. [S+A]
9. **Grabby aliens** (Hanson, Martin, McCarter, Paulson; arXiv 2102.01522).
   - Birth times follow a hard-steps power law. Expansion is at constant
     speed, and births inside already-claimed volume are suppressed.
   - Sonnet: birth time `T0·u^(1/n)` (a sampler it derived itself, medium).
   - Astra simplifies to `A_i(x) = t_i + |x - x_i|/v_i`, processed
     chronologically with ties broken by ID.
   - Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.01522,
     https://arxiv.org/html/2102.01522v3. Confidence: high for the model,
     medium for the simplification. [S+A]
10. **Expansion between galaxies is cheap once interstellar travel is
    solved.** Source:
    https://www.fhi.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/intergalactic-spreading.pdf.
    Confidence: medium. [S]

**Extinction hazards**

11. **Draw each extinction threshold once, then accumulate hazard (Astra).**
    - Set `E_c = -ln(U_c)`, keyed on `(seed, entity, level, cause, episode)`.
      The event fires when the accumulated `∫h ≥ E_c`.
    - This is invariant to how the interval is subdivided.
    - A shared catastrophe is one event with one owner and many consequences.
    - Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-47540-7. Confidence:
      high for the maths, medium for the architecture. [A]
12. **A "time of perils" hazard state machine.**
    - Ord's estimates per 100 years: nuclear war about 1/1000, climate change
      about 1/1000, engineered pandemics 1/30, unaligned AI 1/10, total about
      1/6 (the total only via Sonnet's Wikipedia snippet), natural risks under
      1/2000 (Sonnet).
    - To convert a century probability to a constant hazard:
      `h = -ln(1 - p100)/100`.
    - Sources: https://www.tobyord.com/writing/the-precipice-revisited [A],
      https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Precipice:_Existential_Risk_and_the_Future_of_Humanity
      [S]. Confidence: medium. [S+A]
13. **Natural-extinction upper bounds (Snyder-Beattie, Ord and Bonsall).**
    Below about 1/14,000 and about 1/87,000 per year, at relative-likelihood
    thresholds. These are bounds, not rates. Source: the Nature paper above.
    Confidence: high. [A]

**Logistics, probes and game references**

14. **A minimal logistics model** with population, industrial capacity, fuel
    and maintenance. Launches are limited by
    `min(floor(I/C_I), floor(F/C_F), berths)`. K comes from a fixed, versioned
    planetary sample, never from whichever tiles happen to have been viewed.
    Sources: https://forums.matrixgames.com/viewtopic.php?t=374975 (Distant
    Worlds 2) and the Atlas `place-query-v1.md` and `habitat-suitability.ts`.
    Confidence: medium. [A]
15. **Self-replicating probes as Lotka-Volterra on a network.** Edge flux
    switches on after distance/speed. Source:
    https://github.com/DuncanForgan/lotka-volterra-probes (commit d298536).
    Confidence: high (code read). [A]
16. **FreeOrion routing, fuel, resupply and blockades** as a model for keeping
    settled, reachable, supplied and controlled as separate states. Source:
    https://raw.githubusercontent.com/freeorion/freeorion/master/universe/Fleet.cpp.
    Confidence: high. [A]
17. **4X game references.**
    - Stellaris endgame crises, scaled to empire size (30 to 60% territory
      loss). Source:
      https://guildorder.com/games/stellaris/wiki/end-game-crises (a
      third-party wiki seen as a snippet; medium). [S]
    - Stellaris awakening fallen empires. Source:
      https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?appids=281990&enddate=1476967139
      (failed to open in full; medium). [A]
    - Sins II phase lanes that change with orbits. Source:
      https://www.stardock.com/games/article/527795/sins-ii-dev-journal-13---gameplay-trailer-breakdown
      (high). [A]
    - Aurora 4X hidden jump points, fuel and maintenance. Sources:
      https://aurora4x.com/t/aurora-v1-0-0-launch-documentation/40?page=2,
      https://navalgazing.net/Aurora-Tutorial-Part-5 [S],
      https://aurora2.pentarch.org/index.php?topic=1789.0 (failed to open in
      full) [A]. Confidence: medium.
    - Treat all of these as fiction and design patterns.
18. **Ground truth versus knowledge (Aurora 4X pattern).** The jump or route
    graph is a pure function of the seed; what has been discovered is separate
    epistemic state. Confidence: medium (a design inference). [S]

### Where they agree

- Use a graph of star systems with closed-form or shortest-path arrival
  times, not iterative front simulation in an order-dependent queue.
- Make L and other poorly known parameters **seeded scenario knobs**, not
  truth.
- Ord's numbers are subjective priors, so label them as such.
- Fix a canonical hazard bucket, or a single threshold, and never re-roll it

- Use integer years for travel times.

### Where they conflict or differ

- **Grabby-aliens n range.** Sonnet: "n roughly 3 to 9". Astra, citing the
  full HTML text: "volume-power n = 3 to 12, with 6 as a middle estimate".
  *Astra read the v3 full text, so prefer 3 to 12 (middle 6). Re-check before
  hard-coding.* The paper's exact title was not verified in this synthesis.
- **How to sample a hazard.** Sonnet: a per-century Bernoulli draw on stream
  `(entity, hazard kind, century bucket)`, with survival `(1-h)^n`. Astra: one
  exponential threshold per (entity, cause, episode) against accumulated
  hazard. *Both are deterministic if the bucket is fixed. Only Astra's is
  invariant when time is subdivided, so prefer it. Sonnet's own trap note
  ("fix the canonical bucket, never re-roll") is the condition under which
  its version is safe.*

- **Existential risk versus extinction.** Astra stresses that Ord's figures

  Sonnet used them as "self-extinction" defaults. *Astra's reading is the

## Topic 5: Technology and discovery (Sonnet only)

There is no Astra cross-check for this topic. Treat everything here as
single-source.

1. **Kremer hazard.** The discovery hazard per bucket is
   `λ = a·effective_pop·contact_factor`, accumulated in fixed-point. Isolated
   regions fall behind. Sources: see Topic 2, item 11. Confidence: high.
2. **Henrich critical-population threshold.** Each tech has a complexity, and
   a population below `N*(complexity)` cannot maintain it, so the tech decays
   (the Tasmania pattern). Source:
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3404092 (a review that discusses
   Henrich and Powell, not the original paper). Confidence: high.
3. **The demography-to-complexity link is contested.**
   - Powell et al.'s agent model loses its population dependence when
     learners copy randomly or conformistly.
   - Vaesen et al. (PNAS 2016) argue that population size does not explain
     changes in complexity. Seen as a title only:
     https://abdn.elsevierpure.com/files/100834691/PNAS_2016_Vaesen_E2241_7.pdf.
   - So expose a learning-bias knob. Confidence: medium.
4. **Arthur combinatorial evolution.** Arthur and Polak (2006) grew complex
   logic circuits from a single NAND gate by combination plus selection. The
   proposed use is `hash(world_seed, sorted(component_ids)) < p` for a lazy
   tech graph. Sources:
   https://stias.ac.za/2019/06/combinatorial-evolution-a-theory-of-evolution-for-technology
   (title only), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_evolution (not
   read). Confidence: medium. **The paper itself was not read.**
5. **A hybrid tech tree.** A fixed capability spine (fire through space) with
   combinatorial leaf techs under each tier, as a prerequisite DAG.
   Confidence: low (Civ and Factorio specifics from memory).
6. **Resource-gated tech.** Each tech requires a resource vector supplied by
   Atlas region queries, with a stub planet profile until then. Confidence:
   medium (design synthesis).
7. **A stateless discovery draw.** Use `hash(seed, C, T, b)`. The coarse
   level fixes the discovery bucket, and the fine level refines the date
   within it by inverting the cumulative hazard. Confidence: medium (design).
8. **Diffusion along contact edges** as a second hazard term next to
   invention. Confidence: medium.
9. **Events perturb the shared state variables** (population, contact,
   energy) and never edit the tech tree directly. Confidence: low (design).
10. **Tech-gated self-extinction hazards** (nuclear, climate, pandemic, AI)
    that scale with capability. Confidence: low (speculative design).
11. **A validation harness of statistical signatures.** Hyperbolic early
    growth, loss when population is cut, lag in isolated regions, tier order
    never violated, and digest equality. Confidence: medium.
12. **Energy class as a gate** (Morris, Smil, Kardashev). From memory; Astra's
    Topic 2 sources now supply verified Morris and Kardashev figures.

**Topic 5 traps.**

- A monotone tech tree cannot express collapse.
- Combinatorial explosion (2^n combinations): prune and cap.
- Sort component IDs before hashing a combination.
- Arthur-Polak has no coupling to resources.
- A single global tier hides that societies mix tiers by region and domain, so
  keep a tier per domain.

**Gaps.** Arthur-Polak's selection rules were not read. No open-source
combinatorial tech simulator was found, nor any Factorio or ONI recipe data
beyond https://github.com/factoriolab/factoriolab (MIT, not read). It is
unclear what replaces "fire" as the energy gate on an anoxic planet.

   https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Elite_Dangerous (secondary). Confidence:
   low.
7. **Versioning and registries** (all from memory, low):
   - Stamp a generator version into every digest and save.

   - Content addressing by hash(generator version, seed, query).

not established (an empirical golden test is needed). The canonical
serialisation format is undecided. Space Engine, MakeHuman and Foldit were not
researched.

## Traps (deduplicated)

**Determinism and order**

1. A seeded RNG is not query-order independence. One shared stream, chained
   splits, sequential IDs or discovery-order collections all make history
   depend on who asked first (Talk of the Town, NADSM, Forgan's
   `srand(time(NULL))`). [S+A]
2. `Map`, `Set` and object key order, unstable sorts, and equal sort keys leak
   into state. Sort by a total key with explicit tie-breaks. [S+A]
3. Tuple keys built by plain concatenation can alias. Reusing a key and
   counter repeats a draw. `randomWord % n` is biased. Pin the samplers as
   well as the generator. [A]
4. Observer effect: if zooming, caching or lazy realisation consumes RNG or
   feeds back, history depends on the viewer. [S+A]
5. Optional fine simulation can quietly become causal, for example a
   biography that "supplies the grievance" for a war. Every macro-relevant
   cause must live in the canonical layer. [A]
6. Query-shaped partitions or bridges, built around whatever was asked first,
   change the result. Fix the underlying trees. [A]
7. Changing the generator's configuration changes history. Version it as part
   of the world definition. [A]

**Arithmetic**

8. `Math.sin`, `exp`, `pow` and `log` are not bit-exact across engines.
   Quantise **before** any comparison that decides an event, not only before
   the final digest. [S+A]
9. Numbers above 2^53 are not exact integers. Number bitwise operators are
   32-bit. BigInt division truncates toward zero, so define floor division for
   negative time offsets. [A]
10. Float reassociation changes totals. Fix the reduction order or use
    integer accumulation. [S+A]
11. `Math.imul` returns only the low 32 bits. Philox needs the high word.
    [A]

**Numerics of the models**

12. A reporting interval is not an integration interval: one century can hold
    a collapse and a recovery. [A]
13. Euler steps: logistic is unstable for rh ≥ 2; HANDY needs sub-decade
    steps; SDT has a singularity as K(W) → 0; Kremer blows up in finite time.
    [S+A]
14. Clipping negative stocks to zero manufactures collapses and absorbing
    extinctions. [A]
15. Nonlinear aggregation loses information (the sum of N_i² is not
    (ΣN_i)²). Coarse rates need moments or calibrated closures. [A]
16. `1 - (1-p)^n` describes only fixed, independent, repeated opportunities.
    [A]
17. Tau-leaping can drive counts negative, and an adaptive leap tied to live
    state makes results depend on the level. [S]

**Modelling and science**

18. Rejection sampling can loop forever (DF). It must be bounded and seeded,
    and rejecting every extinction biases the generator. [S+A]
19. Pure rationalisation (Qud) has no causality. Do not let it drive numbers.
    [S+A]
20. Emergent detail is mostly trivia, and more simulated years do not mean
    more interesting history. Curate, and measure variety and causal depth.
    [S+A]
21. Contested theories (SDT, demography and complexity, Diamond's axis) are
    not laws. Seshat's PC1 is descriptive. Eurasian calibrations are not
    planetary laws. [S+A]
22. Fermi-style results are dominated by a few poorly known parameters. Ord's
    figures are subjective, and existential risk is not extinction. [S+A]
23. Independent per-colony hazards overstate diversification when risks are
    shared. [A]
24. A monotone tech tree cannot express collapse or loss. [S]
25. One empire dominating everything needs a modelled counter-process, such
    as dissolution, not arbitrary resets. [A]
26. Missing is not empty: Atlas census code distinguishes incomplete patches
    from complete patches that prove absence
    (`universe-atlas/src/forest-census.ts`). [A]

**Cost and scale**

27. Polling and nested full-population loops are the main performance killers
    (Paradox). [S]
28. Lazy spatial generation does not give cheap lazy causal history, because
    of chain disaggregation and the large causal past in competitive
    expansion. [A]
29. Story sifting can become the bottleneck (Winnow: 912 ms per event at
    1,000 partial matches). [A]
30. Snapshots that cannot be rebuilt from the seed become a hidden second
    source of truth. [S]

**Licences and evidence**

31. GPL (the Neolithic sim, Freeciv, FreeOrion, lotka-volterra-probes),
    CC BY-NC-SA (Seshat) and unclear licences (pyworld3, Felt and Winnow)
    allow re-derivation only. [S+A]
32. WebFetch summaries can paraphrase or hallucinate. Re-check every specific
    number before encoding it. [S]

## What this implies for a deterministic multi-level civilisation simulation

1. **Make one canonical strategic history authoritative.** Make every finer
   level a pure function, `refine(seed, manifest, parentConstraints, address)`,
   that never writes back. Enforce the projection law `A(R(H_c,k)) = H_c` with
   one `node --test` per level edge.
2. **Write the level table before writing code.**
   - Time: epoch, century, decade, year.
   - Space: star graph, system, planet, region cell, settlement.
   - Abstraction: causal state, events, chronicle text, observation indices.
   - Give every fact exactly one owning level. Cross-cell or cross-planet
     events get one canonical owner plus references, as in Minecraft's
     starts and references.
3. **Derive every draw from a counter-based or path-hash RNG.** Key it on an
   unambiguously encoded tuple
   `(seed, modelVersion, entity, level, bucket, purpose, ordinal, attempt)`.
   Never share or chain a stream. Pin the distribution samplers. Check
   reference vectors in tests. Use BigInt or 16-bit halves for 64-bit and
   multiply-high arithmetic.
4. **Keep all authoritative state in integers or fixed-point** on an integer
   time axis aligned with Atlas `world-time`. Use floor division for negative
   offsets and half-open intervals. Quantise at every decision boundary. Use
   no `Math.exp`, `log`, `sin` or `pow` on the authoritative path; use our own
   tables or integer routines instead.
5. **Build the causal core as a small stock-and-flow model per region.** Use
   closed-form updates (exact logistic with a K-crash, exponential capital
   decay, first-order delays, Kremer innovation) at a **fixed internal
   interval** that does not change with zoom. Add faster models (HANDY,
   chiefdom cycling, conquest) only with sub-steps or dated events. A
   reporting interval is never an integration interval.
6. **Schedule events; never poll them.** Each event runs as enumerate
   eligibility, then choose timing, then select the outcome, then validate,
   then commit.
   - The total order key is
     `(time, causalMicrostep, phase, ownerId, eventId)`.
   - Simultaneous intents read one pre-state.
   - Chains are guarded state machines with typed references, lifecycles,
     timeouts and namespaced IDs.
   - Pulses are coarse and differ by level.
7. **Draw each hazard threshold once per (entity, cause, episode)** and fire
   the event when accumulated fixed-point hazard crosses it. This holds for
   extinction, plague, collapse and discovery alike, and is invariant to how
   time is subdivided.
   - Shared catastrophes and Atlas environmental events have a single owner
     and are never re-sampled.
   - Ord and Snyder-Beattie numbers appear only as labelled scenario priors
     and bounds.
8. **Conserve quantities by construction.**
   - Split integer populations with binomial or multinomial trees on frozen
     partition trees.
   - Refine births, deaths and migration jointly, with migration as one
     transfer record.
   - Place events conditionally once their count is fixed.
   - Refine regime and tech stages with endpoint-conditioned paths.
9. **Keep a separate narrative layer.** It holds Qud-style rationalised
   chronicles, Tracery-like text with an injected RNG, and read-only story
   sifting. It runs over the fixed causal log with a salience filter, and it
   can never change outcomes.
10. **Model technology as maintained state per domain, not a monotone tree.**
    - Discovery hazard comes from effective population times contact (Kremer).
    - Loss happens below a complexity threshold (Henrich), with a
      learning-bias knob because the evidence is contested.
    - Planet resource vectors gate techs; until Atlas is wired in, a stub
      planet profile supplies them.
    - A ranked prerequisite DAG with allowed alternatives keeps tiers
      consistent.
11. **Make collapse, regression, extinction and spacefaring explicit outcome
    classes.** Keep biological survival separate from industrial capability
    and political continuity. Never reject a history because it ends in
    extinction. Bound every acceptance test with a seeded attempt counter,
    record the reason for each rejection, and use a versioned fallback.
12. **Draw scenario parameters from wide seeded priors** (Sandberg-style) and
    record them in the manifest and digest. Implement contested theories
    (SDT stress, demography and complexity, Diamond-style connectivity,
    Tainter returns) as swappable strategies registered in sorted ID order.
13. **Expand into space on a seeded star-system graph.**
    - Travel times are integer years. The front speed follows
      `v_f = d/(d/v_p + T_p)`, and arrival is a shortest path with ID
      tie-breaks.
    - Launches belong to the galactic level, and planets only explain them.
    - Exploration and contact (fast) are separate from settlement (slow).
    - Settled, reachable, supplied and controlled are separate states.
    - The Aurora-effect steady state and the Landis clustering are the test
      oracles.
14. **Make digests honest and debug like lockstep.**
    - Keep three digests: the recipe commitment, a Merkle result digest per
      (level, bucket) over RFC 8785-style canonical bytes, and a separate
      materialisation record.
    - Tests rerun one seed under permuted query schedules (coarse-first,
      fine-first, shuffled, cold cache) and find the first interval where
      they diverge.
    - Golden digests sit next to invariant checks and negative tests, because
      a golden digest alone only proves stability.
15. **Treat Atlas as a future pure, versioned input behind a narrow
    interface.** Climate, habitat and resource queries are pinned to an Atlas
    version. "Missing" is distinguished from "empty". Civilisation history is
    exported early as a versioned JSON event log so that other games and
    viewers can build on the file format, as Dwarf Fortress tools did on
    Legends XML.
