### (1) Capacity-aware allocation (`domain.mjs`, ARRIVAL)

*As built in round 3; D5 replaced the split rule (see the D5 amendment). The
probe, tier/culture copy, infrastructure and witness rules below still hold.*

Under `m5-colony-2`, an accepted arrival (founding or reinforcement) is split
across the destination's regions in proportion to the remaining room
`max(0, K − pop)`:

- An empty region's room is evaluated as if it carried the passengers' tiers,
  because it will. A probe copy of the arc is passed through `installEnvironment`.
- A receiving region that was empty takes the voyage's tiers, culture and
  cultureId. A populated region keeps its own.
- A founding gives `10 × share` infrastructure to each receiving region, as the
  default gives `10 × persons` to its one region. A reinforcement adds no
  infrastructure, as in the default.
- An arc with no room anywhere falls back to the default region (first
  `K > 0`, else the first region).
- Witnesses: one `transit.pop → region.pop` transfer effect per receiving
  region. Changed tiers, culture and cultureId are recorded as set effects plus
  `data.sets`, using the same loop as founding. Reinforcement compares against
  the arc before this event, so two arrivals in one tick do not double-record.

The default path keeps `first positive-capacity region` exactly as before. The
landing-region tier copy and the single transfer are unchanged.

### (2) Stranded or diverted voyages (`domain.mjs`, ARRIVAL)

A rejected arrival (`occupied-by-other-lineage` or `destination-uninhabitable`)
under `m5-colony-2` is resolved in this order:

1. **Diverted**, if the voyage has not already been diverted.
   - Candidates: `routesFor` from `m2b.mjs`, evaluated from the rejecting body
     with the voyage's tiers. The voyage knows no signals or failures, so only
     the stellar-doom clause and hosting/tier feasibility apply.
   - Candidates must have travel time ≤ 500 years at the voyage's transport
     tier, and cannot be the rejecting body itself.
   - Ranking, with no RNG: `habitable` before `habitat-buildable`, then the
     shorter travel time, then the body id.
2. **Stranded**, if the voyage has not already been stranded, carries habitat
   tiers (Energy ≥ 5 and Subsistence ≥ 5), and the rejecting body's star has
   not reached its doom year. The crew waits 500 years at the same body, then
   retries its arrival once.
3. **Otherwise** it dies exactly as in the default: `turned-away` or `lost`,
   a `-persons` transit effect, and `lastDeath`.

Each rescue creates a new voyage leg, `<voyage id>~diverted` or
`<voyage id>~stranded`:

- It has the same record shape (`VOYAGE_FIELDS`), lineage, tiers and culture.
- Its `launchEventId` is the rejection event, so the next arrival's
  `EXOGENOUS` cause points at the rejection.
- It gets its own transit account, ARRIVAL message and `VOYAGE_LOST` hazard.
  The ordinary transport-tier hazard applies while it is under way or waiting.
- The id suffix enforces the limits: at most one diversion and one stranding
  per voyage. A chain therefore has at most three arrivals.

The rejection event stays a `COLONY_FOUNDED` with `outcome: 'rejected'` and the
same reason. Its stage becomes `DIVERTED` or `STRANDED`. Its effects are one
`transit.pop → transit.pop` transfer to the new leg's account, which also goes
into the D ledger's `transfers`, plus `voyage.status` set to `diverted` or
`stranded`. A `voyage` ref points to the new leg. No death is recorded, the
occupant still receives the physical-contact SIGNAL, and the lineage still gets
the `TARGET_FAILURE` report. All writes are fields that `COLONY_FOUNDED`
already declares (`transit.pop`, `voyage.status`), so the catalogue,
`validateEvent` and the contract digest are untouched.

### (3) Arriving specialists (`domain.mjs`; `maintenancePopulation` unchanged)

*D5: reinforcements only. A founding arrival creates no roster entry.*

Under `m5-colony-2`, every accepted arrival adds its passengers to a
STATUS-shaped roster entry for the destination arc:

- source `<arc id>#specialists`;
- `qualified[d][k] = persons if voyage.tiers[d] ≥ k`;
- `tiers` = the voyage's tiers;
- `reportYear` = the arrival year;
- `routeDelayYears` = 0.

The entry is delivered into that arc's inbox in the same tick. The planet's
existing STATUS handler records it in `arc.lineageReports` with its own witness
effects, caused by the arrival event.

`maintenancePopulation` then credits it like any fresh own-lineage report under
a colony policy. The credit is bounded:

- **Amount:** 100/1000 of `qualified[d][region tier]`.
- **Time:** 300 years (`STATUS_STALE_YEARS`).
- **Qualification:** only domains where the specialists' tier is at least the
  region's tier.
- **One slot per arc:** arrivals in the same tick are summed, and a later
  arrival tick replaces the slot. This is conservative: earlier specialists
  lose their remaining window rather than extending it.

This is the only state the policy adds. No new arc field is needed, and
`COLONY_ARC_FIELDS` and `WITNESS_VERSION` are unchanged.

## Open risks

- **Founding split weakens small colonies.** *Resolved by D5 (founders stay
  together; see the amendment). Original text:* Splitting a founding party evenly
  by room moved the disconnected-network TECH_LOSS from 3850 (m5-colony-1) to
  3420. Eight 500-person regions maintain less than one 4000-person region.
  This follows the brief literally ("distribute arriving persons ... replacing
  first-positive-region"). A bounded alternative is to keep founding parties
  together and split only reinforcements, or to set a minimum share per region.
  That is a design decision for Fable/Kasper, and it must be measured before
  either option is made the default.
- **Diversion uses only static knowledge.** It ranks by declared routes,
  hosting, the passengers' tiers and stellar doom. It can divert into another
  occupied body, as the graph(4) chain shows; it is then stranded once and
  lost. That keeps it free of omniscience, but in crowded domains it may
  rescue fewer passengers than an informed choice would.
- **Stranding costs nothing in fuel or infrastructure**, and the retry ignores
  any change in foreign occupancy other than the occupant actually being gone
  at the retry. The 500-year windows are [scenario] values with no source.
- **The specialist slot replacement** can shorten an earlier roster's credit
  when a later reinforcement arrives. This is deliberate (conservative), but it
  means the order of arrivals matters.

## D5 amendment: founders stay together

**Decision D5, Opus-decided on Kasper's instruction (overrulable), 2026-10-07.**
Fix the colony policy before anyone measures it. Branch
`m5/colony-2-semantics`, worktree `civilization-lab-wt/colony-2-semantics`,
based on `main` at e6b2fd6. m5-colony-2 was changed in place: it is opt-in,
unmeasured and unreleased.

**Why.** Founders' knowledge already is the colony: it sets `foundingTiers` and
the region tiers, and it gets `reserveInitialYears`. Crediting the same people
again as "arriving specialists" counted them twice. Splitting 4000 founders into
eight 500-person regions created below-viable outposts, and since
`REGRESSED_STABLE` fires on any region, every split was penalised. That is what
moved the network fixture's first TECH_LOSS from 3850 to 3420.

**Rules now (`src/domain.mjs` `capacityShares`, `src/tech.mjs` `COLONY_M5_2`).**

- Room is `max(0, K − pop)` per region, with empty regions judged with the
  passengers' tiers (the round-3 probe).
- Arrivals go first to regions that already have people, split by largest
  remainder in proportion to remaining room (ties by region id). If those
  regions cannot take everyone, each is filled to its room.
- Only the overflow goes to empty regions, one at a time, in m5-colony-1's
  landing order: the region m5-colony-1 would pick (first `K > 0`, else the
  first region), then region id. A region is filled before the next is started.
- Anything left when every region is full goes to that landing region.
- For a founding (no populated regions) this means: fill the landing region,
  spill only when it is full. The landing-region tier copy that m5-colony-1
  does before `installEnvironment` is now also done under m5-colony-2, so a
  founding party that fits in one region is placed byte-for-byte as under
  m5-colony-1.
- The specialist roster is filled only when the colony already exists
  (reinforcements). A new colony gets no `#specialists` STATUS.
- `COLONY_M5_2` descriptors: `allocation` is now
  `founders-together-reinforcements-by-remaining-room` and `specialists` is
  `reinforcement-status-roster`. The version string is unchanged, so the
  m5-colony-2 manifest and recipe digests differ from round 3's. Default and
  m5-colony-1 are untouched.

**Tests (`tests/m5-colony-2.test.mjs`, still 8 tests).**

- Test 1: the m5-colony-2 reserve-cliff `resultDigest` now equals the
  m5-colony-1 golden `78aeff32…507b`. The negative control moved to a
  reinforced fixture, where the roster still changes the history.
- Test 2: founding → `[r00, 4000]`; 2000-person reinforcement →
  `[r00, 2000]` with no region sets; 200000-person reinforcement →

  `r00 243, r01–r07 251` on reinforcement, starting seven empty regions each
  time.
- Test 3 renamed from "unsupported reserve cliff: no hidden survival boost;
  …" to "unsupported reserve cliff ages unchanged; disconnected network founds
  and loses tech exactly as m5-colony-1 (D5)". On the network fixture, the
  colony after the founding tick and the founding event are
  `canonicalString`-equal to m5-colony-1's. Repinned: first TECH_LOSS after the
